# Review date provenance audit

The supplied review pickles provide `review date(months ago)` and no absolute publication, scrape, first-observed, or snapshot timestamp columns. The old BERT notebook reads an absent `df_reviews.pkl`, partitions on this relative age, and scores older reviews; it does not record the reference date. The SARIMA notebook reads absent `levis_reviews.pkl` and `df_test_results.pkl` files from Google Drive, then also works with relative ages. The Data Reader notebook only exports a derived product table. No source code in this repository reconstructs exact review dates.

Across **81,377 reviews**, there are only **60 distinct relative-age values**. The older slice uses integer months from 12 to 660; the recent slice spans 0.07 to 11 months, with 1,725 fractional-age rows overall. The concentration at 12, 24, 36, and later multiples of 12 suggests coarse year-based display or conversion for many records. Three rows exceed 20 years and one says 660 months (55 years); those require source-level inspection and should not be turned into dated observations. Text may have been posted or scraped at a different time than its displayed age.

The files' filesystem modification times are around **2025-03-23 UTC**. A modification time can reflect copying or serialization and is not proof of when the reviews were collected or when “months ago” was measured. Even assuming that reference date would only place many reviews in broad intervals, not specific weeks. Converting `12 months ago` to one day or one Sunday would manufacture precision and could move reviews across train/test boundaries.

The machine-readable [date audit](review-date-audit.json) records counts and the file times without assigning absolute review dates. Current disposition: the pickles can support a limited, clearly labeled **rating-proxy text exercise** after deduplication, but they cannot supply time-correct weekly review features or a verified temporal holdout. Recover the original scrape output, page timestamps, source URLs, and collection logs; otherwise collect dated reviews prospectively. If only coarse ages are available, a monthly interval-censored exploratory analysis is possible, but it would be a different research design with far fewer independent periods.
